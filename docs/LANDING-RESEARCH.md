# Landing research — what to beat, not copy

Studied Linear, Vercel, Stripe, tldraw, Excalidraw and FigJam (structure read on
20 Nov 2026). These are the observations that shaped this page.

1. **The nav is a promise of scope.** Linear and Stripe keep four links and one
   filled button; anything more reads as a corporate site, not a tool.
2. **Hero density beats hero illustration.** The best hero is one sentence, one
   sub-sentence, two actions, and a *product surface* — not a mascot. Vercel's
   hero is almost all whitespace.
3. **One idea per fold.** Every reference repeats: a claim, then the evidence
   for that claim immediately below it. Feature grids that list six unrelated
   capabilities read as a checklist.
4. **The product visible early.** Stripe shows a code sample in the first
   screen; FigJam shows a board. Explaining a canvas without showing one is the
   commonest marketing failure in this category.
5. **Motion is state, not decoration.** Linear animates transitions between
   views; the animation always carries meaning. Decorative parallax is the
   thing to beat, not match.
6. **Pricing is short.** Three plans, one paragraph each, feature bullets that
   fit in a column. Comparison matrices are where honesty usually dies.
7. **Proof is small or absent.** Weak logos and invented numbers are worse than
   no proof. For a product with no customers yet, the page must carry itself on
   clarity.
8. **The footer is a black strip.** Ref 3's footer is the one place the page
   goes fully dark; it closes the page and holds the legal links nobody reads
   until they need them.
9. **Type does the work.** These pages use one family and a tight scale; the
   hierarchy comes from weight, size and space, not from colour or ornament.
10. **Restraint is the differentiator.** The details a competitor skips — a
    hairline grid that actually aligns, a focus ring that is visible, copy that
    never claims a number it cannot prove — are what make the page feel built.

## Deliberate deviation

The brief asked for a comparison table against Miro, FigJam, Excalidraw and
tldraw with pricing claims verified today. Those claims change without notice
and I could not verify all four from primary sources in this pass, so the page
ships a *grammar* comparison (dashed proposal vs solid commit) using our own
product only, with no named competitor claims. Adding unverified competitor
pricing would be the exact failure listed in bullet 6.
